TeX (s1.tex:740–750): "Let n be an integer, 0 ≤ δ, p ≤ 1, X ∼ Bin(n,p) and μ := E X = np. Then

### s1:citThm21 — `LovaszStatement` (`EG/Spec/Ext/Lovasz.lean`)

TeX (s1.tex:1015–1019): "Every n-vertex graph can be decomposed into at most n/2 paths and
cycles."

Lean: for every `H : FGraph V` (finite, simple, loopless) there are lists `P`, `C` with
`IsPathCycleDecomp ↑E(H) P C` (paths: `≥ 2` vertices, no repeated vertex; cycles: `Obj.WF`, i.e.
no repeated vertex and `≥ 3` vertices; the edge lists concatenate to a duplicate-free list whose
members are exactly `E(H)`) and `2(|P| + |C|) ≤ |V(H)|` (⇔ `|P| + |C| ≤ ⌊n/2⌋`). This is
Lovász's theorem (1968: every graph on `n` vertices is the union of at most `⌊n/2⌋` edge-disjoint
paths and cycles) with `n` counting isolated vertices and for simple graphs — the safe direction
on both counts flagged by LOV-TRUTH (a multigraph or loop version would be false). As a stage-α
hypothesis this statement is trusted; I find it correct. Universe-polymorphic in `V` (LOV-UNIV).
